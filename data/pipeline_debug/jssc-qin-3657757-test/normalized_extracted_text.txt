Abstract— This work proposes a wideband low phase noise
(PN) quad-core quad-mode voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO)
which features magnetically reconﬁgurable (MR) technique and
fully symmetrical architecture. The MR technique, realized
through a transformer-based tank integrating an embedded
switched inductor, leverages varying core excitation currents to
achieve four distinct equivalent inductances, thereby enabling
quad-mode operation. This approach avoids the frequency tuning
range (FTR) compromise encountered when using capacitive
tuning elements in multi-mode tanks. Furthermore, the fully sym-
metrical characteristics of the inductive, capacitive, and active
elements, along with the routing layout, minimize mismatches
among the VCO cores, ensuring effective synchronization of
the four single-core VCOs and optimizing the PN in quad-core
operation. The proposed quad-core quad-mode VCO achieves an
FTR of 113.2% (4.21 to 15.18 GHz), a PN of −141.4 dBc/Hz at
a 10 MHz offset from 4.28 GHz, a peak ﬁgure-of-merit (FoM) of
190.5 dBc/Hz, a peak FoMT of 211.6 dBc/Hz and a peak FoMTA
of 220.5 dBc/Hz at a 10 MHz offset.
Compared to the scheme that uses multiple individual
VCOs for multi-band operation, mode-switching technique
summarized in [1] provides a more efﬁcient solution, enabling
multi-band coverage with reduced area and lower power con-
sumption. The switched-inductors or switched-transformers
techniques are favorable to achieve wide bandwidth by utiliz-
ing multiple effective inductance values [2], [3], [4]. Although
these techniques can broaden the bandwidth effectively in a
simple manner, the ON-resistance of the switches degrades the
Q factor of the tank, thus signiﬁcantly deteriorating the PN.
Recently, coupling multiple VCO cores operating at different
modes via mode switches has been demonstrated to be a
promising method to simultaneously achieve low PN and wide
FTR.
Index Terms— Magnetically reconﬁgurable (MR), phase noise
(PN), quad-core, quad-mode, voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO),
wide frequency tuning range (FTR).

(denoted “E”) to realize dual-mode operation. While an octave
tuning range can theoretically be divided or multiplied to
target any frequency band, dual-mode VCOs typically struggle
to achieve full octave coverage due to practical limitations.

In multi-core multi-mode VCOs, transformer-based res-
onant tanks are preferred for their design ﬂexibility and
area efﬁciency. However, as the number of operation modes
increases, transformer design becomes challenging and the
coupling network becomes complex, both of which tend to
introduce asymmetries across VCO cores. As a result, the
asymmetry induced by transformers or even other components
inevitably introduces frequency mismatches in multi-core
operation, which theoretically degrade the PN performance.
Therefore, a fully symmetrical architecture in multi-core multi-
mode VCOs are preferred to eliminate the negative impact on
the PN performance.
A substantial number of state-of-the-art triple-mode or
quad-mode VCOs have been proposed in recent years. A dual-
core triple-mode VCO with a wide FTR of 80.6% and a PN of
−137.9 dBc/Hz at a 10 MHz offset from 7.15 GHz is proposed
by using Electrical-Magnetic (E-M) mixed coupling in [15].
However, the asymmetrical inductance seen from each core
causes inter-core mismatches that degrades the PN. Moreover,
the inclusion of additional mode switches and switched capac-
itor arrays (SCAs) impose extra parasitics, thus reducing the
FTR. A quad-core quad-mode VCO in [22] achieves an FTR
of 101% and a PN of −133.2 dBc/Hz at a 10 MHz offset
from 13.7 GHz by using E-M mixed coupling, whereas the
issue of asymmetrical inductance persists, and the parasitics
from electric coupling capacitors negatively impact the FTR.
Additionally, the non-uniformity of the FTR across each mode
is also undesirable. With the same total FTR, a uniform FTR

This gives rise to two concerns. First, large differences in

mode VCOs have demonstrated superior performance in terms
of FTR and PN, issues such as asymmetrical inductance,
parasitic-induced FTR reduction, and nonuniform FTR still
persist, ultimately degrading the overall performance of the
VCOs.
To surmount these issues, this work proposes a quad-
core quad-mode VCO that achieves ultra-wide bandwidth and
low PN by employing MR technique and fully symmetric
architecture, which effectively eliminates the asymmetry and
extra parasitics, and ensures a uniform FTR across all modes.
This article is organized as follows. Section II presents the
structure of the proposed VCO and the operating principles
of the four modes. Section III discusses the symmetry of
the design and its optimization of performance. Section IV
presents the measurement results and Section V draws the
conclusion.

mismatches among four cores. In a multi-core VCO,
all cores should be typically designed to be identical to
ensure balanced operation. This mismatch in inductance
will lead to unequal current distribution among the
cores, which in turn causes imbalances in the waveforms
among VCO cores. Then the current will ﬂowthrough
the lossy switches in order to equalize the energy of
the resonators, thereby forcing the VCO to oscillate at
the same frequency. This process, however, degrades the
tank Q and renders the theoretical 6 dB PN optimization
from the quad-core operation unachievable. Therefore,
this mode is abandoned.
In summary, three distinct equivalent inductance values
are realized through a fully symmetrical transformer
network composed of four mutually coupled inductors.
However, since the difference Lmode,L −Lmode,M exceeds
Lmode,M −Lmode,H by 4Mopp, achieving continuous
frequency tuning between mode-M and -H may result
in a frequency gap between mode-L and -M. Therefore,
an additional mode inserted between mode-L and -M is
required, which should minimally affect other modes.
B. Quad-Mode Operating Principle

distinct modes-mode-1, -3, and -4, as shown in Fig. 6(a),
(c), and (d), operate following the same principle as

mode-L, -M, and -H, respectively.

inductive, capacitive, active, and other elements in the quad-
core quad-mode VCO, the mismatches among VCO cores are
signiﬁcantly minimized, allowing the four single-core VCOs to
be well synchronized and achieve optimized PN in all modes.
B. Avoiding Mode Ambiguity
It is known that when the transconductance pairs are sufﬁ-
ciently large, the oscillator operates at the frequency where the
tank impedance provides sufﬁciently high amplitude with zero
phase. When no mode switches are added or the switch size is
too small [i.e., large ON-resistance (RON)], multiple frequency
points with high impedance amplitude and zero phase may
exist. As a result, the operating frequency of the oscillator is
not uniquely deﬁned, resulting in mode ambiguity.
Due to the symmetrical property of the proposed trans-
former, the tank impedance seen from node pairs P1 and
P2, P3 and P4, P5 and P6, P7 and P8 is identical. Fig. 14
shows the simulated magnitude and phase responses of the
tank impedance seen from the node pair P1 and P2 in the four
operation modes, as RON is reduced from 425 to 25 . When
RON is 225/425 , as shown by the blue/green dotted curve
in Fig. 14, each mode exhibits at least two frequency points
where the tank impedance has a sufﬁciently high peak and a
zero phase. This implies that the oscillator has the potential to
oscillate either at the frequency with the expected maximum
Q value or concurrently at multiple resonance frequencies,
which are highly undesirable.
To avoid the issues associated with ambiguous modes, it is
necessary to reduce RON by increasing the size of the mode
switches. As shown by the solid red curves in Fig. 14, when
RON is reduced to 25 , each mode exhibits a uniquely deﬁned
resonant frequency.

C. Effect of Mismatches Among Cores
While ideal synchronization assumes identical oscillation
frequencies among the cores, practical implementations often
suffer from frequency mismatches caused by device variations
and layout asymmetries. These mismatches introduce phase
errors and amplitude reduction, ultimately degrading the PN
performance [21], [27]. Therefore, accurately characterizing
and mitigating inter-core mismatches are essential to fully
unlock the potential of multi-core VCO designs.
The proposed design makes every effort to ensure symmetry
among the four VCO cores, including the transformer tank,
SW
array, VCO cores and layout routings. As presented in
Fig. 9(a), each single tank inductor is identical and arranged
in a centrally symmetric layout. Additionally, the SW array,
consisting of sixteen switches, is also arranged in a center-
symmetric conﬁguration, as illustrated in Fig. 15. That is to
say, each port (P1−8) is identically connected to four switches
to ensure that the parasitics induced by the switches is the
same across all ports. Moreover, each VCO core is connected
to the SW array by identical routings, and synchronized with
other three cores directly through the SW array, thus further
reducing frequency mismatches and mitigating its impact on
the VCO performance.

IV. MEASUREMENT RESULTS
The proposed quad-core quad-mode VCO is implemented
in 65 nm bulk CMOS with a core chip area of 0.152 mm2,
as shown in Fig. 21. Fig. 22 shows the measured PN at 4.28,
8.66, 11.57, and 15.07 GHz, respectively, from four distinct
modes. The measured FTR is 113.2% (4.21–15.18 GHz), with
the frequency in each mode ranging from 4.21 to 6.24 GHz,
5.62 to 8.66 GHz, 7.52 to 11.6 GHz, and 10.01 to 15.18 GHz,
respectively, as shown in Fig. 23. In each mode, the tuning
voltage ranges from 0 to 2.5 V. It’s worth noting that the
minimum frequency overlap between two adjacent modes is
10.5%, which further validates the robustness of the wide
bandwidth. We further report the VCO gain KVCO on this
plot at Vtune = 1.2 V . Fig. 24(a) and (b) show the measured
PN, FoM and FoMT over the whole FTR, respectively, where
the PN is from −108.8 to −120.3 dBc/Hz and −130.7 to
−141.4 dBc/Hz at 1 and 10 MHz offsets while the FoM
and FoMT vary from 180.0 to 190.5 dBc/Hz and 201.1 to
211.6 dBc/Hz at a 10 MHz offset.
The tank’s effective parallel resistance RT varies by mode,
owing to variations in Q across modes. Since mode-1 exhibits
the largest Q and thus the largest RT , it requires the smallest
gm and hence the lowest current consumption, while the
higher frequency and lower Q modes require more. In the
proposed design, a programmable tail resistor array is used
to adjust the current. In mode-1, a larger tail resistor is
used to moderate gm, while smaller resistors are employed
in modes-2 and -4 to allow higher gm. The VCO consumes
1.44 to 14.12 mW power under a VDD of 0.4 V, with
mode-1: 1.44–3.27 mW; mode-2: 9.51–13.46 mW; mode-3:
11.14–14.10 mW; and mode-4: 12.63–14.12 mW. Although
the 0.4 V low supply voltage helps reduce intrinsic power
consumption of the VCO, an on-chip regulator is typically
required to suppress noise and improve the power supply
rejection ratio (PSRR), which results in additional power
overhead. Across different modes, the Kpush of the proposed
VCO is measured to range from 126 to 297 MHz/V. Since
spur level depends on the PSRR, an on-chip regulator with
high PSRR is required to keep spurs low. Although magnetic
coupling brings Q variations, the tail resistor array ensures the
robust start-up and also suppresses ﬂicker noise up-conversion
across a wide FTR. This effect is reﬂected in the 1/f3 PN
corners in each mode, which span 35–220 kHz (mode-1), 130–
210 kHz (mode-2), 180–600 kHz (mode-3), and 90–750 kHz
(mode-4). A lower corner frequency indicates more effective
suppression of ﬂicker noise up-conversion. Thanks to the tail
resistor, the normalized PN (to 1 GHz) shows good ﬂatness
with minimal variations across the whole FTR, as shown in
Fig. 24(a). Moreover, Fig. 25 shows that the proposed VCO
exhibits the best overall performance in view of FoM and
FTR, and the best FoMTA in similar operating frequencies. The
results are compared with state-of-the-art wideband VCOs in
Table I, where the proposed VCO exhibits the largest FTR and
the largest FTR overlap facilitated by MR technique, validating
the robustness of the ultra-wide bandwidth.
V. CONCLUSION
In this article, we present a quad-core quad-mode VCO
featuring MR technique and fully symmetrical topology.
By changing the current directions of the tank inductors
via the mode-switching array and incorporating the switched
inductor, four distinct inductance values are realized, enabling
quad-mode operation without electrical coupling. Besides,
with careful consideration of the symmetry in the inductive,
capacitive, and active elements, as well as the routing layout,
mismatches among the VCO cores are signiﬁcantly minimized,
resulting in optimized PN performance. The VCO achieves
an FTR of 113.2% (4.21-to-15.18 GHz), a peak FoMT of
211.6 dBc/Hz and a peak FoMTA of 220.5 dBc/Hz at a 10 MHz
offset.
