Abstract— The rapid adoption of electric vehicles (EVs) has
heightened the need for advanced diagnostic frameworks to
ensure the safety, reliability, and longevity of battery sys-
tems. Batteries, as the cornerstone of EV performance, are
susceptible to capacity degradation, thermal instability, and
anomalies that compromise efﬁciency and safety. Traditional
diagnostic approaches, relying on physics-based models and
heuristic thresholds, often fail to address the complexity and
variability of real-world EV operations, necessitating scalable,
efﬁcient, and privacy-preserving solutions. This paper presents
a Split Multi-Task Federated Learning (SMTFL) framework
that leverages a Transformer-based architecture to address the
dual challenges of battery health anomaly detection and capacity
estimation. By combining the efﬁciency of split learning with the
collaborative beneﬁts of federated learning, the proposed system
enables EVs to train and share partial model updates while
retaining sensitive data locally. Experimental results on a recent
large-scale EV dataset demonstrate the superior performance
of the SMTFL framework, achieving an accuracy of 98.9% for
battery health anomaly detection and a mean absolute error
(MAE) of 0.32 Ah for capacity estimation, corresponding to a rel-
ative error of approximately 1.80%, signiﬁcantly outperforming
state-of-the-art approaches. By training a single model for both
tasks and performing computations during charging sessions, the
framework minimizes energy consumption and computational
overhead, ensuring practicality for large-scale deployment in
real-world EV systems.
In this work, we distinguish between battery health anomaly
detection and capacity estimation, two related yet distinct
diagnostic tasks. Following established battery diagnostics
conventions [1], State of Health (SoH) refers to the overall
degradation of the battery (often expressed as a percentage
of initial capacity), whereas capacity estimation quantiﬁes the
absolute remaining charge in ampere-hours (Ah). Accordingly,
we frame health monitoring as a binary anomaly detection task
(normal vs. anomalous conditions such as lithium plating or
thermal runaway), and capacity estimation as a regression task.
The complexity of battery diagnostics stems from the highly
variable operating environments of EVs [2], which involve
ﬂuctuating charging and discharging cycles, environmental
conditions, and aging effects. Traditional methods, such as
physics-based models or predeﬁned threshold-based diagnos-
tics, struggle to adapt to such real-world complexities, often
leading to suboptimal performance. Moreover, the integration
of EVs into connected vehicular networks introduces addi-
tional constraints, including the need for scalable solutions that
can handle large data volumes while preserving data privacy.
Index Terms— Electric vehicle, battery state of health, state of
charge (SOC), battery management system (BMS), split federated
learning, transformer.

II. RELATED WORK
Battery diagnostics and capacity estimation are critical
research areas for ensuring the safety, reliability, and oper-
ational efﬁciency of electric vehicles (EVs). Early approaches
often relied on statistical models and traditional machine
learning techniques, such as statistical distribution analysis
and canonical algorithms, to diagnose faults and detect anoma-
lies [5], [6]. More recently, Li et al. [7] review artiﬁcial neural
network (ANN) approaches for state-of-health (SoH) estima-
tion and remaining useful life (RUL) prediction, discussing
battery aging mechanisms, available datasets, and ANN archi-
tectures, and outlining key challenges related to deployment
feasibility and model interpretability.
Sultan et al. [8] propose an active cell balancing system to
address imbalanced state of charge (SoC) in EV lithium-ion
battery packs caused by cell inconsistencies. The study eval-
uates seven machine learning models for predicting battery
lifespan and remaining useful life (RUL). Das et al. [9]
estimate the state of health (SoH) of lithium-ion batteries
in electric vehicles using machine learning algorithms like
KNN, SVR, DT, and RF, based on direct battery measurements
(voltage, current, temperature). The study proposes collabo-
ration with hardware manufacturers for better battery health
monitoring systems.
The advent of deep learning introduced advanced mod-
els [10], such as long short-term memory (LSTM) networks,
capable of capturing temporal dependencies and improving
anomaly detection in battery systems [6], [11]. However,
the development of robust deep learning methods hinges on
the availability of large-scale, publicly accessible datasets.
In response, recent advancements in EV battery research
have produced datasets tailored to various tasks, including
anomaly detection, capacity estimation, and remaining useful
life (RUL) prediction. For instance, datasets such as Sultan
et al.
[8] and HUST [12] focus on controlled experimental
conditions, while large-scale datasets like EVBattery [1] pro-
vide diverse real-world charging data. Additionally, specialized
datasets address manufacturing defects through CT scans [13]
or explore capacity estimation using voltage relaxation pro-
ﬁles [14].
Xiong et al. [15] propose a lithium-ion battery health
prognosis method using real-world BMS in electric vehi-
cles. The approach combines partial charge voltage curves as
health indicators with a moving-window-based RUL prediction
model. While the method is well-suited for many applica-
tions, its reliance on linear degradation models may present
challenges when applied to batteries exhibiting complex degra-
dation patterns.

battery health and capacity estimation. This uniﬁed
approach, facilitated by split learning, simpliﬁes deploy-

locally while ofﬂoading the remaining computations to
the edge server. This minimizes computational demands
on EVs and ensures scalability for real-world applica-
tions.
• Introducing a privacy-preserving and scalable federated
learning architecture that integrates differential privacy
mechanisms to protect sensitive data. The framework
facilitates seamless collaboration among EVs, edge
servers at charging stations, and a global parameter server,
ensuring efﬁcient training across EVs while maintaining
model consistency and safeguarding sensitive data within
the EVs.
• Demonstrating the practicality and effectiveness of the
proposed solution through rigorous validation on a recent
and realistic, large-scale EV dataset, achieving superior

Deng et al. [16] propose a rapid health estimation frame-
work for in-service EV battery packs that combines a
digital-twin model based on an equivalent-circuit represen-
tation, feature extraction from short charging segments, and
deep convolutional neural networks with transfer learning
and domain adaptation to cope with limited labeled ﬁeld
data. Han et al. [17] emphasize the signiﬁcance of dynami-
cally reconﬁgurable battery management systems (RBSs) for
enhancing fault tolerance, improving charge and temperature
balancing, and achieving greater energy efﬁciency and opera-
tional ﬂexibility. While the proposed approach offers valuable
advancements, the implementation of RBSs may encounter
challenges such as the computational demands of optimization
algorithms, increased hardware design requirements, and prac-
tical considerations like managing transient behaviors during
switch operations.
Liu et al. [18] tackle the challenge of battery state of health
(SoH) estimation under limited sample conditions by intro-
ducing a reﬁned composite multiscale discrete sine entropy
(RCMDSE) algorithm. Similarly, Kumari et al. [19] propose
a hybrid approach for estimating state of charge, health,
and temperature (SoC, SoH, SoT). Their method combines
an emperor penguin-based residual network-110 (R110) with
bidirectional long short-term memory (BLSTM) networks.
This approach demonstrates signiﬁcant promise, achieving
high accuracy on EV drive cycles simulated in MATLAB.
However, the reliance on advanced optimization techniques,
such as simulated annealing, may limit its real-time applica-
bility in resource-constrained environments. Kant et al., [20]
proposed a method that integrates a bidirectional recurrent
neural network and long-short-term memory (biRNN-LSTM)
to improve the prediction of the state of health (SoH) by
identifying complex patterns and improving accuracy.
Despite notable advancements in battery diagnostics and
capacity estimation, several limitations persist within the
state of the art. Approaches targeting complex degrada-
tion behaviors often fail to capture nonlinear dynamics [8],
while recent innovations [17], [19] face challenges related
to computational overhead, limiting real-time applicability
in resource-constrained EV. Many methods rely on datasets
generated under controlled laboratory conditions [6], [11],
restricting their generalizability to diverse real-world sce-
narios. Moreover, existing techniques often assume that EV
data can be centrally collected, overlooking critical privacy
concerns.
Federated learning (FL) has recently gained traction as
a distributed training paradigm in a variety of application
domains. For instance, Wu et al. [21] employ federated deep
reinforcement learning to perform multi-objective optimization
in STAR-RIS-aided satellite–aerial–ground integrated net-
works (SAGINs). In the context of battery health monitoring
and state-of-health (SoH) estimation, several studies [22],
[23], [24] have leveraged FL-based designs. Abbaraju and
Kundu [22] propose a federated ensemble learning (FEL)
approach that combines federated learning and ensemble learn-
ing to enhance SoH estimation. Their solution relies on an
LSTM model that is updated after each trip, leveraging het-
erogeneous data sources such as trafﬁc conditions and weather.
Wang et al. [23] introduce an adaptive multi-personalized fed-
erated learning (AMFL) algorithm that reﬁnes SoH estimation
by dynamically adjusting local models through personalized
layers and client clustering while incorporating a differential
privacy mechanism. Similarly, Wong et al. [24] develop a
decentralized federated learning approach for Li-ion battery
SoH forecasting, featuring an improved client selection strat-
egy and real-world validation experiments.
Despite notable progress, existing battery diagnostic meth-
ods exhibit several limitations. Model-based approaches often
rely on simpliﬁed linear degradation assumptions [15], failing
to capture complex real-world aging dynamics. Federated
learning (FL) methods for SoH estimation [22], [23], [24]
reduce data centralization but impose signiﬁcant computational
burdens on resource-constrained EVs and lack energy-efﬁcient
training schedules, which could impact battery autonomy.
Moreover, they typically focus on a single task (e.g., SoH
estimation), requiring separate models for different diagnostics
and increasing system complexity. Many methods are also
evaluated on controlled laboratory datasets [6], [11], limiting
their applicability to diverse real-world conditions.
To address these gaps, we propose a Split Multi-Task
Federated Learning (SMTFL) framework that: (1) employs
a Transformer-based encoder to model nonlinear temporal
dependencies; (2) reduces on-device computation by ofﬂoad-
ing heavy layers to edge infrastructure via split learning,
where only early layers are trained locally; (3) incorporates
differential privacy mechanisms to protect sensitive battery
data during model updates; (4) uniﬁes battery health anomaly
detection and capacity estimation into a single model, eliminat-
ing the need for separate, task-speciﬁc models and optimizing
parameter efﬁciency; (5) schedules training exclusively during
charging sessions to preserve EV battery life; and (6) is
validated on the large-scale real-world EVBattery dataset [1]
to ensure practical relevance. This innovative framework
effectively balances scalability, privacy preservation, and com-
putational efﬁciency, addressing the key challenges identiﬁed
in the state of the art.
III. SMTFL FRAMEWORK
Our framework integrates split learning and federated
architectures for battery health monitoring, balancing local
computation with global performance while incorporating
differential privacy. The following sections detail the archi-
tectural components, including the split Transformer model,
privacy-preserving training protocol, and federated coordina-
tion strategy.
A. System Model
The proposed system employs a hierarchical federated
learning architecture that integrates three primary components:
electric vehicles (EVs) as clients, edge servers deployed at
charging stations, and a global parameter server in the cloud.
The workﬂow is designed to ensure synchronization, prevent
version conﬂicts, and facilitate efﬁcient and scalable model
training. Figure 1 illustrates the interactions and hierarchical
structure among these components. At the start of each global

C. Split Federated Training
The client-side model processes raw battery data locally,
transforming it into intermediate representations (smashed
data). To preserve privacy, Gaussian noise is added to the
smashed data before transmission, ensuring differential pri-
vacy. The noisy smashed data are then sent to the edge server,
which completes the forward pass using its layers, computes
the multi-task loss, and backpropagates gradients through its
own layers and back to the smashed data. These gradients
are returned to the EV, which uses them to update its local
layers via backpropagation. After completing a ﬁxed number
of local epochs, the EV uploads its updated client-side weights,
again applying differential privacy by adding Gaussian noise,
to the edge server for aggregation. This design reduces the
computational load on EVs while safeguarding data privacy
and ensuring scalable, distributed training.

A. EVBattery Dataset
We evaluate our framework using the EVBattery dataset [1],
a large-scale real-world benchmark for EV battery diagnos-
tics. The dataset comprises charging snippets collected under
diverse conditions—including fast/slow charging and varying
temperatures—from three anonymized manufacturers. Each
record contains 128-step time-series of eight features (voltage,
current, temperature, SOC, etc.) and meta-information (vehicle
ID, mileage, snippet index). Binary health labels (0: normal,
1: anomaly) and continuous capacity labels (28.28–46.23 Ah)
accompany each sample. A full description is given in Table I;
additional dataset speciﬁcs are available in the original publi-
cation [1].
The cleaned dataset contained no missing values. To preserve
real-world conditions, the natural class imbalance was main-
tained for anomaly detection; in Non-IID splits, each client
was guaranteed at least three-batch-size samples per class to
prevent extreme skew, without any synthetic resampling. This
approach ensures a realistic evaluation of the model under
practical data distributions.
The dataset was prepared from raw .pkl ﬁles containing
multivariate time-series data and metadata. Sequences were
converted into NumPy arrays, and entries with missing or
invalid values were discarded. Valid capacity labels (non-zero)
were retained as regression targets, while anomaly labels were
encoded as binary values (0: normal, 1: anomalies such as
lithium plating or thermal runaway). For normalization, all
time-series features were standardized per feature (zero mean,
unit variance) using a StandardScaler ﬁtted exclusively
on the training set. Capacity labels were similarly normalized.

approximately 2L2d operations per forward pass. Accounting

and ∼10 s per round, each round consumes 0.083 Wh. A 20-

a typical fast-charging session (10 kWh). Consequently, the
framework introduces negligible communication and energy
overhead while preserving the EV’s primary function.

V. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
This paper introduced a Split Multi-Task Federated Learning
(SMTFL) framework for joint battery health anomaly detec-
tion and capacity estimation in electric vehicles (EVs). The
proposed approach combines a Transformer-based encoder
with a split federated architecture to achieve state-of-the-art
performance while addressing critical challenges in scala-
bility, privacy, and energy efﬁciency. Experimental results
demonstrate that our framework outperforms existing methods,
achieving an accuracy of 98.9% for anomaly detection and a
MAE of 0.32 for capacity estimation. These results validate the
model’s capability to capture complex temporal dependencies
and feature interactions across both tasks. By enabling EVs to
train a single uniﬁed model locally and ofﬂoading heavy com-
putations to edge servers, the SMTFL framework signiﬁcantly
reduces on-device resource requirements. Moreover, privacy
is preserved through differential privacy mechanisms and split

learning, while training is scheduled exclusively during charg-
ing sessions to minimize energy overhead. Collectively, these
attributes make the SMTFL framework a practical and efﬁcient
solution for large-scale, privacy-aware EV battery diagnos-
tics. Future work will focus on extending the framework to
additional battery management system (BMS) tasks, such as
predicting battery lifespan and identifying speciﬁc component
failures. We also plan to explore adaptive split points that
dynamically adjust the computation ofﬂoading based on device
capabilities and network conditions, as well as design more
sophisticated multitask models that integrate a broader set of
BMS functions without compromising efﬁciency.
